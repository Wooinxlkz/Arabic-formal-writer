# Formality Tiers within MSA

Not all formal Arabic is equally formal. Using the wrong tier is a more common real-world mistake than using dialect — it's the difference between a document that's "correct but stiff" or "correct but too casual for the context."

## Tier 1 — Administrative / Institutional (الأعلى رسمية)
**Use for**: government correspondence, formal complaints, legal-adjacent documents, contracts, addressing officials.

- Full honorific titles every time the recipient is referenced
- No contractions of any kind, no rhetorical questions, no first-person informal transitions
- Passive voice preferred for actions/decisions (تم اتخاذ القرار not اتخذنا القرار, when the actor is the institution rather than "I")
- Closing formula from the top of the `regional-conventions.md` closing list (#2 or #3)

## Tier 2 — Professional / Business (رسمي متوسط)
**Use for**: business letters between companies, cover letters, professional emails, reports to a manager, CVs.

- Honorific on first reference, name alone acceptable after
- Active voice is fine and often clearer
- Can use "we/I" directly (نحن/أنا) rather than defaulting to passive
- Warmer opener acceptable (يسعدني أن أتقدم بطلب... in a cover letter, for instance) — Tier 1's coldness would feel wrong here
- Closing formula #1 or #2

## Tier 3 — Formal but Personal (رسمي دافئ)
**Use for**: a formal-register message to someone you have an actual relationship with — a professor you know, a colleague at another company, a formal thank-you note.

- Still zero dialect, still standard grammar
- Can open with warmer greetings, closer to natural spoken rhythm within MSA's boundaries
- Closing formula #1, sometimes #4

## How to pick

Ask: **what is the power/distance relationship, and is this a one-off or a first contact?**
- Unknown institution, first contact, or any legal/administrative weight → Tier 1
- Known business relationship, job application, non-personal professional context → Tier 2
- Existing personal-professional relationship → Tier 3

If genuinely unsure, Tier 2 is the safer default — Tier 1 in the wrong context reads as cold/robotic, Tier 3 in the wrong context reads as presumptuous. Tier 2 rarely offends either direction.
